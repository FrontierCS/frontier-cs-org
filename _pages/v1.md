      <div class="meta"><span class="tag">Problem 0</span><span>Packing</span></div>
      <h3>Polyomino Packing</h3>
      <p>Pack every piece, rotated or reflected as you like, into the smallest rectangle. Every packing without overlap is valid, and the score is the share of the rectangle the pieces fill.</p>

<section class="sec" id="research">
  <h2>In research</h2>
  <p class="tbd">Because the score keeps moving, other groups use FrontierCS to measure what more compute buys and as a signal to train on.</p>
  <div class="uses"><h3>Test-time scaling</h3><div class="rows">
    <article class="row"><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15309" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">When Agents Slow Down: Understanding LLM Agents&rsquo; Test-Time Strategies via Elo-per-token Analysis</a></h4><p class="au">Liu, Mang, Peng, Chai, Li, Pimpalgaonkar, Zettlemoyer, Dimakis, Cheung</p><p>Tracks the best solution at every token budget in sessions of up to 100M tokens. On Polyomino Packing, splitting 100M tokens across parallel sessions gains 264 Elo over one long session.</p></article>
    <article class="row"><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21032" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Scaling Discovery through Test-Time Communication</a></h4><p class="au">Park, Kontonis, Garg, Krishnamurthy, Papailiopoulos</p><p>Compares a team of agents that share findings with the best of as many independent agents. On Polyomino Packing the team reaches 0.945, above the best score reported before it.</p></article>
  </div></div>
  <div class="uses"><h3>Search scaffolds</h3><div class="rows">
    <article class="row"><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20133" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">AdaEvolve: Adaptive LLM Driven Zeroth-Order Optimization</a></h4><p class="au">Cemri, Agrawal, Gupta, Liu, Cheng, Mang, Naren, Erdogan, Sen, Zaharia, Dimakis, Stoica</p><p>Compares evolutionary search methods on 172 FrontierCS problems with 50 GPT-5 calls each. AdaEvolve averages 61.33, against 50.75 for OpenEvolve and 20.64 for a single call.</p></article>
  </div></div>
  <div class="uses"><h3>Training</h3><div class="rows">
    <article class="row"><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36802" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">EasyPPO: Stabilizing the Critic Is Key</a></h4><p class="au">Zhou, Mang, Mao, Li, Chai, Mishra, Wang, Narasimhan, Cheung, Gonzalez</p><p>Uses the continuous score as a reinforcement-learning reward and validates on the algorithmic problems. Its best validation score is 14.89% above PPO&rsquo;s.</p></article>
    <article class="row"><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29082" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Evolution Fine-Tuning: Learning to Discover Across 371 Optimization Tasks</a></h4><p class="au">Lee, Kim, Kang, Chuen, Chen, Han, Jung, Kang</p><p>Turns evolutionary search runs into training data; 172 of its 371 tasks derive from FrontierCS. On held-out problems its 9B model averages 46.01, against 32.46 for the base model.</p></article>
    <article class="row"><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14445" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">FrontierSmith: Synthesizing Open-Ended Coding Problems at Scale</a></h4><p>Generates open-ended training problems from closed-ended ones and measures the gain on FrontierCS: 8.82 points for a 9B model and 12.12 for a 27B model.</p></article>
  </div></div>

<section class="sec" id="questions">
  <div class="faq">
    <h2>Questions</h2>
    <div class="fqlist">
      <details><summary>What makes a problem open-ended?</summary><div class="ans"><p>Its checker accepts any valid answer and then measures how good it is. No known answer is optimal, so a score of 100 marks a strong reference and not the end of the problem.</p></div></details>
      <details><summary>How do the two leaderboards differ?</summary><div class="ans"><p>In a single attempt, a model writes one program and that program is scored. An agent works in a sandbox through Harbor for up to 5 hours, can submit many times for feedback, and keeps its best score.</p></div></details>
      <details><summary>How do I run a problem?</summary><div class="ans"><p>Clone the repository, run <code>uv sync</code>, then score a solution with <code>frontier eval algorithmic 0 your_solution.cpp</code>. To run an agent, use <code>uv run frontier harbor trial algorithmic 0 -a codex -m gpt-5.5</code>. The test cases are public.</p></div></details>
      <details><summary>Where are the research problems?</summary><div class="ans"><p>Research and repository-level tasks continue in <a href="{{ '/' | relative_url }}">FrontierCS 2</a>. This page covers the algorithmic problems.</p></div></details>
