<section class="sec" id="problems">
  <h2>Problems</h2>
  <div class="probs">
    <article class="prob">
      <figure class="ptile"><canvas id="pktile" role="img" aria-label="A packing of 300 pieces tightening as a search runs"></canvas><figcaption id="pklabel"><b></b></figcaption></figure>
      <h3>Polyomino Packing</h3>
      <p>Pack every piece, rotated or reflected as you like, into the smallest rectangle. Every packing without overlap is valid, and the score is the share of the rectangle the pieces fill.</p>
      <div class="lk"><a href="https://github.com/FrontierCS/Frontier-CS/blob/main/algorithmic/problems/0/statement.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Statement</a><a href="https://github.com/FrontierCS/Frontier-CS/tree/main/algorithmic/problems/0/examples" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Example solutions</a></div>
    </article>
    <article class="prob">
      <figure class="ptile"><canvas id="wmtile" role="img" aria-label="Two valid maps of the same 14 countries, the second much smaller"></canvas><figcaption id="wmlabel"><b></b></figcaption></figure>
      <h3>World Map</h3>
      <p>Given which of N countries share a border, colour a K by K grid so that two colours touch exactly when their countries are neighbours. Any such map is valid, and a smaller map scores higher.</p>
      <div class="lk"><a href="https://github.com/FrontierCS/Frontier-CS/blob/main/algorithmic/problems/6/statement.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Statement</a><a href="https://github.com/FrontierCS/Frontier-CS/blob/main/algorithmic/problems/6/chk.cc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Checker</a><span>Adapted from <a href="https://qoj.ac/download.php?type=statement&amp;id=13904&amp;ver=hr_hr" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">IOI 2025</a></span></div>
    </article>
    <article class="prob">
      <figure class="ptile"><canvas id="pmtile" role="img" aria-label="A hidden permutation of eight numbers uncovered query by query"></canvas><figcaption id="pmlabel"><b></b></figcaption></figure>
      <h3>Permutation</h3>
      <p>A permutation is hidden. Each query is a sequence, and the only answer is how many positions match. Any strategy that finds the permutation is valid, and fewer queries score higher.</p>
      <div class="lk"><a href="https://github.com/FrontierCS/Frontier-CS/blob/main/algorithmic/problems/2/statement.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Statement</a><span>Adapted from <a href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/combinatorics-probability-and-computing/article/mastermind-with-a-linear-number-of-queries/3BCAC12ED935616D36871C12E10A2288" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Mastermind with a linear number of queries</a></span></div>
    </article>

<section class="sec" id="research">
  <h2>In research</h2>
  <p class="tbd">Other teams have picked up FrontierCS for their own work. Some use it to see how far an agent gets with more time and tokens, some to compare search methods, and some to train models on it.</p>
  <div class="uses"><h3>Test-time scaling</h3><div class="rows">
    <article class="row"><div><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15309" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">When Agents Slow Down: Understanding LLM Agents&rsquo; Test-Time Strategies via Elo-per-token Analysis</a></h4><p class="au">Liu, Mang, Peng, Chai, Li, Pimpalgaonkar, Zettlemoyer, Dimakis, Cheung</p><p>Tracks the best solution at every token budget in sessions of up to 100M tokens. On Polyomino Packing, splitting 100M tokens across parallel sessions gains 264 Elo over one long session.</p></div><img class="pg1" src="{{ 'assets/img/fcs1-papers/2609.15309.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="" width="440" height="570" loading="lazy"></article>
    <article class="row"><div><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21032" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Scaling Discovery through Test-Time Communication</a></h4><p class="au">Park, Kontonis, Garg, Krishnamurthy, Papailiopoulos</p><p>Compares a team of agents that share findings with the best of as many independent agents. On Polyomino Packing the team reaches 0.945, above the best score reported before it.</p></div><img class="pg1" src="{{ 'assets/img/fcs1-papers/2609.21032.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="" width="440" height="570" loading="lazy"></article>
    <article class="row"><div><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07447" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Fork-and-Flush: Escaping Idea Basins in Autoresearch Agents</a></h4><p class="au">Cai, Ziakas, Kontonis, Pearce, Krishnamurthy, Garg, Papailiopoulos</p><p>Periodically forks an agent&rsquo;s run and clears its context so it leaves a plateau. Nine of its thirteen tasks come from FrontierCS; its normalized average is 0.78, against 0.47 for a single run.</p></div><img class="pg1" src="{{ 'assets/img/fcs1-papers/2610.07447.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="" width="440" height="570" loading="lazy"></article>
    <article class="row"><div><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07625" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Stateless Language Agents: Scaling Long-Horizon Automated Research</a></h4><p class="au">Zhang, Ji, Sun, Li, Upasani, Ruan, Ma, Hong, Kamanuru, Lee, Mao, Zhang, Shao, Mang, Dimnaku, Hu, Poovendran, Olukotun</p><p>Keeps the research state in the harness and builds a fresh context for every agent call. On a FrontierCS algorithm-design problem it scores 59.5 at 200M tokens, against 58.0 for the strongest baseline.</p></div><img class="pg1" src="{{ 'assets/img/fcs1-papers/2610.07625.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="" width="440" height="570" loading="lazy"></article>
  </div></div>
  <div class="uses"><h3>Search scaffolds</h3><div class="rows">
    <article class="row"><div><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20133" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">AdaEvolve: Adaptive LLM Driven Zeroth-Order Optimization</a></h4><p class="au">Cemri, Agrawal, Gupta, Liu, Cheng, Mang, Naren, Erdogan, Sen, Zaharia, Dimakis, Stoica</p><p>Compares evolutionary search methods on the FrontierCS algorithmic problems with 50 GPT-5 calls each. AdaEvolve averages 61.33, against 50.75 for OpenEvolve and 20.64 for a single call.</p></div><img class="pg1" src="{{ 'assets/img/fcs1-papers/2602.20133.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="" width="440" height="570" loading="lazy"></article>
    <article class="row"><div><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23413" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">EvoX: Meta-Evolution for Automated Discovery</a></h4><p class="au">Liu, Agarwal, Maheswaran, Cemri, Li, Mang, Naren, Boneh, Cheng, Pan, Du, Keutzer, Cheung, Dimakis, Sen, Zaharia, Stoica</p><p>Evolves the search strategy during a run as well as the solutions. On the FrontierCS algorithmic problems, with 100 iterations each, it reaches a mean of 62.6 and a median of 75.5.</p></div><img class="pg1" src="{{ 'assets/img/fcs1-papers/2602.23413.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="" width="440" height="570" loading="lazy"></article>
    <article class="row"><div><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01658" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CORAL: Towards Autonomous Multi-Agent Evolution for Open-Ended Discovery</a></h4><p class="au">Qu, Zheng, Zhou, Yan, Tang, Ong, Hong, Zhou, Jiang, Kong, Zhu, Jiang, Li, Wu, Low, Zhao, Liang</p><p>Replaces fixed evolutionary rules with long-running agents that share memory. On Polyomino Packing, four agents reach 89.4% coverage, against 56.0% for a single attempt.</p></div><img class="pg1" src="{{ 'assets/img/fcs1-papers/2604.01658.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="" width="440" height="570" loading="lazy"></article>
    <article class="row"><div><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00675" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">LabBook: Harnessing Experimental History for Efficient LLM-Driven Discovery</a></h4><p class="au">Yuan, Ye, Zhao, Moukheiber, Kautz, Zhang, Chen</p><p>Reuses the history of earlier experiments to guide the search. On 49 FrontierCS algorithmic problems its mean best score is 6.13 points above the strongest baseline with DeepSeek V4 Flash.</p></div><img class="pg1" src="{{ 'assets/img/fcs1-papers/2610.00675.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="" width="440" height="570" loading="lazy"></article>
  </div></div>
  <div class="uses"><h3>Post-training</h3><div class="rows">
    <article class="row"><div><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36802" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">EasyPPO: Stabilizing the Critic Is Key</a></h4><p class="au">Zhou, Mang, Mao, Li, Chai, Mishra, Wang, Narasimhan, Cheung, Gonzalez</p><p>Uses the continuous score as a reinforcement-learning reward and validates on the algorithmic problems. Its best validation score is 14.89% above PPO&rsquo;s.</p></div><img class="pg1" src="{{ 'assets/img/fcs1-papers/2609.36802.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="" width="440" height="570" loading="lazy"></article>
    <article class="row"><div><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29082" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Evolution Fine-Tuning: Learning to Discover Across 371 Optimization Tasks</a></h4><p class="au">Lee, Kim, Kang, Chuen, Chen, Han, Jung, Kang</p><p>Turns evolutionary search runs into training data; nearly half of its 371 tasks derive from FrontierCS. On held-out problems its 9B model averages 46.01, against 32.46 for the base model.</p></div><img class="pg1" src="{{ 'assets/img/fcs1-papers/2606.29082.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="" width="440" height="570" loading="lazy"></article>
    <article class="row"><div><h4><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14445" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">FrontierSmith: Synthesizing Open-Ended Coding Problems at Scale</a></h4><p>Generates open-ended training problems from closed-ended ones and measures the gain on FrontierCS: 8.82 points for a 9B model and 12.12 for a 27B model.</p></div><img class="pg1" src="{{ 'assets/img/fcs1-papers/2605.14445.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="" width="440" height="570" loading="lazy"></article>

<section class="sec" id="questions">
  <div class="faq">
    <h2>Questions</h2>
    <div class="fqlist">
      <details><summary>What makes a problem open-ended?</summary><div class="ans"><p>Its checker accepts any valid answer and then measures how good it is. No known answer is optimal, so a score of 100 marks a strong reference and not the end of the problem.</p></div></details>
      <details><summary>How do the two leaderboards differ?</summary><div class="ans"><p>In a single attempt, a model writes one program and that program is scored. An agent works in a sandbox through Harbor for up to 5 hours, can submit many times for feedback, and keeps its best score.</p></div></details>
      <details><summary>How do I run a problem?</summary><div class="ans"><p>Clone the repository and install it with <code>uv sync</code>. Score a solution of your own:</p><pre>frontier eval algorithmic 0 your_solution.cpp</pre><p>Or let an agent work on the problem:</p><pre>uv run frontier harbor trial algorithmic 0 -a codex -m gpt-5.5</pre><p>The test cases are public.</p></div></details>
      <details><summary>Where are the research problems?</summary><div class="ans"><p>Research and repository-level tasks continue in <a href="{{ '/' | relative_url }}">FrontierCS 2</a>. This page covers the algorithmic problems.</p></div></details>
    </div>
